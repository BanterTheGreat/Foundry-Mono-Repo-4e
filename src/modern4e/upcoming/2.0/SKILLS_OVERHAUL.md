# Skills handoff (2026-11-23)

Modern 4e 2.0 would give every character a way to use what they know when talking to NPCs. Charisma helps when a character relies on charm, deception, or authority. Other skills help when the approach calls for their expertise. The rulebook and Character Builder still use the current skill list.

## Proposed skill list

The proposed list has 15 skills. It adds Streetwise and renames Society to Culture. It drops Diplomacy and Perception, replaces Intimidate with Command, and renames Thievery to Finesse and Bluff to Deception.

### Physical

| Skill | What it?s for |
| --- | --- |
| Athletics | Climb, jump, swim, or vault over obstacles. |
| Acrobatics | Keep your balance, tumble, or slip free of a restraint. |
| Endurance | Keep going through fatigue, harsh weather, or other physical strain. |

### Intrigue

| Skill | What it?s for |
| --- | --- |
| Stealth | Move quietly, stay out of sight, or spot someone who is trying to avoid detection. |
| Finesse | Pick locks, pick pockets, or use careful hand movements to disable traps. |

### Expertise

| Skill | What it?s for |
| --- | --- |
| Arcana | Recognize magic and work out how spells, wards, and magical objects behave. |
| Crafting | Make, repair, or examine objects, structures, and mechanisms. Use it to work out how something was built or spot signs of a hidden mechanism. |
| Medicine | Treat injuries, identify illnesses, and recognize physical traces like blood or signs of a wound. |
| Nature | Read terrain and weather, follow tracks, or recognize plants and animals. This includes reading natural features and hazards underground. |
| Culture | Know the history, customs, laws, and institutions of peoples and nations. Use it to recall a foreign capital, recognize a regiment?s badge, or understand the rules of a royal court. |
| Religion | Recognize gods, religious symbols, rites, and beliefs. Use it to understand what a practice means to the people who follow it. |

### Social

| Skill | What it?s for |
| --- | --- |
| Insight | Read someone’s emotions or intentions from what they say and do. Use it to pick up on hesitation, discomfort, or where their attention keeps going. |
| Streetwise | Find people and information through informal local networks. Use it to work out where to ask or whom to approach, even in a settlement you?ve just arrived in. |
| Deception | Get someone to act on a false belief through a lie, a cover story, or a misleading impression. |
| Command | Get someone to act right away through authority, resolve, or a credible threat. Use it to direct people in a crisis, rally them, or demand immediate compliance. |

## Choose the ability for the approach

When a character makes a check, the GM chooses the skill that fits what they’re doing and the ability that fits how they’re doing it. Add the usual +5 if the character is proficient in that skill, whichever ability the check uses.

For example, use Stealth with Dexterity to slip past a lookout and Stealth with Wisdom to spot a lookout who’s trying to stay hidden. Use Command with Charisma to give a convincing order or Command with Strength to back up a threat with a show of force.

Use Athletics with Strength to climb a rope, a Strength check to force a jammed gate open by brute force, or Endurance with Constitution to hold it open while the party passes through.

**Ability checks.** If the outcome is uncertain and no skill fits the approach, the GM calls for a check using the ability that best fits what the character is doing.

## How to handle social checks

**Persuasion.** There’s no dedicated persuasion skill. The GM picks the skill that best fits what the character does. Use Culture with Intelligence to judge an offer by local trade customs. Use Streetwise to find a broker who might know who is buying. If the NPC’s response is clear from what the character says or offers and what matters to the NPC, go with that response. If the response is uncertain and the character is relying on sincerity, warmth, or flattery, make a Charisma check. For example, use Charisma to see whether a suspicious guard believes your sincere explanation for being there.

**Deception.** Use Deception when you want someone to act on something false they believe. Claiming to be an inspector is a Deception check. If their decision comes down to whether a forged seal looks real, roll Crafting with Intelligence. A successful Deception can keep someone cooperating for as long as they believe the lie.

**Command.** Use Command to get someone to act under pressure. You might direct a panicked crowd off a burning pier, rally a wavering crew through sheer resolve, or threaten a guard into stepping aside. The GM can choose another ability if that’s where the pressure comes from. If you bend a gate’s bars to show you can back up a threat, you could roll Command with Strength. A success can get someone to act or comply right away. For a longer commitment, use the persuasion rules above.

## How to handle noticing and searching

Give characters whatever their senses and actions plainly reveal. When a creature is trying to stay hidden, characters can use Stealth with Wisdom to spot it, even if they were going about their business. Use the creature’s Stealth result as the DC if it rolled to hide. For other discoveries, choose a skill based on what the character examines and how they search. Ask what someone trained in that skill would pick up on.

| Situation | How to resolve it |
| --- | --- |
| Gunmen are hiding behind open windows as the party walks past. | Use Stealth with Wisdom against the gunmen’s Stealth result to see whether the party catches a glimpse in time. |
| A camouflaged drow is hiding against a cave wall. | Use Stealth with Wisdom to spot the drow, or Nature if a character deliberately studies the cave wall for breaks in the rock’s natural pattern. The drow’s Stealth can set the DC. If a lantern held at an angle reveals an obvious shadow, give the discovery outright. |
| A character examines a worked dungeon wall for a hidden mechanism. | Use Crafting to spot uneven mortar joints or wear around a moving panel. |
| A character checks the floor for traps. | Point out plainly visible clues, such as a wire crossing a doorway. Use Crafting to find a disguised pressure plate by examining the floor, and Finesse to disarm it if that takes careful handwork. |
| A character searches a warehouse for a smuggler’s storeroom. | Use Culture to work out where smugglers might keep goods based on which areas customs officers inspect. Use Streetwise to find a dockworker who can point you to the crew that unloaded the goods. Use Crafting to find a hidden room by comparing the inside and outside walls. |
| A suspect has blood on their gloves. | If the blood is visible and recognizable, tell the PCs. Use Medicine to identify an ambiguous stain they can see. If the suspect keeps the gloves out of sight, use their Stealth to resolve whether they manage it. Insight can reveal that the suspect is deliberately keeping their hands out of view. Getting a clear look may settle the question. The blood alone gives the PCs a clue. |

## When implementation is approved

Update the rulebook’s skill-check rules and the Character Builder’s skill list. Check backgrounds, feats, sources of proficiency, and skill utility powers for references to Diplomacy, Intimidate, Perception, Thievery, Bluff, and Society. For each one, decide which skill fits, if any. Review passive detection and Stealth rules that reference Perception.

Decide what happens to existing Diplomacy and Perception picks and to Command picks that would give a character a proficiency they already have. The proposal for skill proficiency ranks is separate.
