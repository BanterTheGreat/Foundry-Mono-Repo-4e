# Skills handoff (2026-11-23)

Modern 4e 2.0 would give every character a way to use what they know when talking to NPCs. Charisma helps when a character relies on charm, deception, or authority. Other skills help when the approach calls for their expertise. The rulebook and Character Builder still use the current skill list.

## Proposed skill list

The proposed list has 14 skills. It drops Diplomacy and Perception, replaces Intimidate with Command, and renames Thievery to Finesse and Bluff to Deception. All checks use skills.

| Skill | Default ability | What it’s for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Athletics | Strength | Climb, jump, swim, or use your strength to get past a physical obstacle. |
| Acrobatics | Dexterity | Keep your balance, tumble, or slip free of a restraint. |
| Stealth | Dexterity | Move quietly, stay out of sight, or spot someone who is trying to avoid detection. |
| Finesse | Dexterity | Pick locks, pick pockets, or use careful hand movements to disable traps. |
| Endurance | Constitution | Keep going through fatigue, harsh weather, or other physical strain. |
| Arcana | Intelligence | Recognize magic and work out how spells, wards, and magical objects behave. |
| Crafting | Intelligence | Make, repair, or examine objects, structures, and mechanisms. Use it to work out how something was built or spot signs of a hidden mechanism. |
| Medicine | Intelligence | Treat injuries, identify illnesses, and recognize physical traces like blood or signs of a wound. |
| Society | Intelligence | Recall the history, customs, and institutions of a place or group. Use it to navigate formal etiquette, understand military ranks and doctrine, or work out how local trade and social networks operate. |
| Insight | Wisdom | Read someone’s emotions or intentions from what they say and do. Use it to pick up on hesitation, discomfort, or where their attention keeps going. |
| Nature | Wisdom | Read terrain and weather, follow tracks, or recognize plants and animals. This includes reading natural features and hazards underground. |
| Religion | Wisdom | Recognize gods, religious symbols, rites, and beliefs. Use it to understand what a practice means to the people who follow it. |
| Deception | Charisma | Get someone to act on a false belief through a lie, a cover story, or a misleading impression. |
| Command | Charisma | Get someone to act right away through authority, resolve, or a credible threat. Use it to direct people in a crisis, rally them, or demand immediate compliance. |

## Choose the ability for the approach

Each skill has a default ability for its most common use. When a character makes a check, the GM chooses the skill that fits what they’re doing and the ability that fits how they’re doing it. Add the usual +5 if the character is proficient in that skill, whichever ability the check uses.

For example, use Stealth with Dexterity to slip past a lookout and Stealth with Wisdom to spot a lookout who’s trying to stay hidden. Use Command with Charisma to give a convincing order or Command with Strength to back up a threat with a show of force.

## How to handle social checks

**Persuasion.** There’s no dedicated persuasion skill. The GM picks the skill that best fits what the character does. Bartering usually calls for Society with Intelligence. If the character is proficient in the chosen skill, add the usual +5. If no skill fits, resolve the appeal through what the character says or offers and what matters to the NPC.

**Deception (Charisma).** Use Deception when you want someone to act on something false they believe. Claiming to be an inspector is a Deception check. If their decision comes down to whether a forged seal looks real, roll Crafting with Intelligence. A successful Deception can keep someone cooperating for as long as they believe the lie.

**Command (usually Charisma).** Use Command to get someone to act under pressure. You might direct a panicked crowd off a burning pier, rally a wavering crew through sheer resolve, or threaten a guard into stepping aside. The GM can choose another ability if that’s where the pressure comes from. If you bend a gate’s bars to show you can back up a threat, you could roll Command with Strength. A success can get someone to act or comply right away. For a longer commitment, use the persuasion rules above.

## How to handle noticing and searching

Give characters whatever their senses and actions plainly reveal. When a creature is trying to stay hidden, characters can use Stealth with Wisdom to spot it, even if they were going about their business. Use the creature’s Stealth result as the DC if it rolled to hide. For other discoveries, choose a skill based on what the character examines and how they search. Ask what someone trained in that skill would pick up on.

| Situation | How to resolve it |
| --- | --- |
| Gunmen are hiding behind open windows as the party walks past. | Use Stealth with Wisdom against the gunmen’s Stealth result to see whether the party catches a glimpse in time. |
| A camouflaged drow is hiding against a cave wall. | Use Stealth with Wisdom to spot the drow, or Nature if a character deliberately studies the cave wall for breaks in the rock’s natural pattern. The drow’s Stealth can set the DC. If a lantern held at an angle reveals an obvious shadow, give the discovery outright. |
| A character examines a worked dungeon wall for a hidden mechanism. | Use Crafting to spot uneven mortar joints or wear around a moving panel. |
| A character checks the floor for traps. | Point out plainly visible clues, such as a wire crossing a doorway. Use Crafting to find a disguised pressure plate by examining the floor, and Finesse to disarm it if that takes careful handwork. |
| A character searches a warehouse for a smuggler’s storeroom. | Use Society to work out where smugglers might keep goods based on which areas customs officers inspect. Use Crafting to find a hidden room by comparing the inside and outside walls. |
| A suspect has blood on their gloves. | If the blood is visible and recognizable, tell the PCs. Use Medicine to identify an ambiguous stain they can see. If the suspect keeps the gloves out of sight, use their Stealth to resolve whether they manage it. Insight can reveal that the suspect is deliberately keeping their hands out of view. Getting a clear look may settle the question. The blood alone gives the PCs a clue. |

## When implementation is approved

Update the rulebook’s skill-check rules and the Character Builder’s skill list. Check backgrounds, feats, sources of proficiency, and skill utility powers for references to Diplomacy, Intimidate, Perception, Thievery, and Bluff. For each one, decide which skill fits, if any. Review passive detection and Stealth rules that reference Perception.

Decide what happens to existing Diplomacy and Perception picks and to Command picks that would give a character a proficiency they already have. The proposal for skill proficiency ranks is separate.
