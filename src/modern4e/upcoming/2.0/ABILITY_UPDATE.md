# Ability Advancement

Spread out the ability increases from level 11 onward. This replaces the old level 16 increase to all six abilities with smaller gains.

| Level | Ability increase |
| --- | --- |
| 11, 16, 21 | Choose two different non-main abilities and increase each by 1. |
| 14, 18, 28 | Increase your main ability by 1. |
| 24 | Increase one non-main ability by 1. |

You can choose the same pair at levels 11, 16, and 21, so one non-main ability can gain up to 3 points by level 21. Keep the existing ability rules for levels 1–10.

## Why change it?

The old level 16 rule raised all six ability modifiers at once. Against our vanilla 4e example, which starts with a final main score of 18 (+4) and keeps raising its main and secondary scores, that put Modern 4e four points ahead at level 16 and five ahead at level 20.

Spreading those gains out keeps the largest single Modern increase to two points. Modern still leads by three points at levels 18–20, and both builds reach 19 total modifier points at level 21 and 21 at level 30.

Choosing two distinct abilities at each milestone gives players room to specialize. A player can keep raising the same secondary ability and gain up to +3 in it between levels 10 and 21, matching the gain in the vanilla example. They can also spread the increases across more abilities if that suits the character.
