The Price of a Way Out

Validated: Normal, above the party's baseline budget. Four level-11 PCs; encounter level 12 (+1); 2,700 XP / 2,400 XP budget. No calculator warnings. Elevation, wet terrain, enemy control and capture pressure add difficulty beyond this enemy-only estimate; they are not priced as extra XP. Assumes the party begins with normal encounter resources.

The gate officer's description and the burner's directions lead the new constables up the charcoal road to an old charcoal clearing, ringed by lichen-furred boundary stones, beside a rain-swollen woodland stream. The mole has hired Mara Reedvein, a Cloudwood guide with a thornwood staff, to take him over the wooded hills and out of Flint; the green cords on their wrists mark travellers under her escort. He is running from everyone now: the RHC, Kell, and the Obscurati courier he robbed. He has no protector, only a purse, a pack and a stolen ring. Mara has since learned her passenger is a wanted constable and is holding out for a higher price. That argument is why the party can catch up. Mara knowingly escorts fugitives for money. Her fellow druid Orris Fen was told the man is fleeing corrupt authorities, and the two hired trail-hands only want their pay. The bramble guardians are bound to Mara by a promise of protection from loggers. A guide's waypoint cache holds the green cords, travelling cloak and route notes the mole needs, with his pack cinched to it: Grappa's address, a copy of the forged guard reassignment, the courier's rendezvous instructions and his final payment. The party can break through a mobile woodland screen, cut off the exit, shake the less committed escorts with proof, or convince the terrified mole that custody is safer than running. The clearing, guides and identities are original additions to your campaign.

Rain rattles through the canopy and drips from the lichen-furred boundary stones. Across the stream, a man in a constable's coat clutches a purse while a woman winding fresh thorns around her staff holds out her other hand. “The price changed when I saw your badge,” she tells him. Then she notices you. “And now it changes again.” A nearby tree unfolds a hand. The man looks at your badges, then at the trail behind him.

Enemy objectives

Mara wants to deliver her paying passenger and refuses to hand him over; she fights on until she falls or the mole is secured. The bramble guardians fight beside her. Orris and the trail-hands are the less committed escorts: they can be shaken by proof (see Shaken escorts) and withdraw rather than die for a stranger's coin. Once Mara falls or the mole is secured, all surviving escorts stand down or flee, and any surviving guardian halts and waits with its branches lowered. The mole surrenders under the conditions below and never fights to the death.

Tactics

Mara speaks for the guides, insisting the constables have no authority “past that stream” and that the mole’s fare is paid for, while Orris watches their flanks. One guardian contests the plank crossing while the other holds the southern ascent to the eastern bank; they advance to threaten movement with Entangling Branch, rather than standing together and sealing the whole stream. Their long reach and bramble auras make a frontal rush expensive, but the broad ford lets the constables split their approach and force the trees to choose whom to stop.

Mara uses Entangle on someone trying to reach the mole, then a Wall of Thorns to bend the pursuit toward a guardian. Orris changes into a lean, moss-backed hound and uses Lunge to slide enemies toward the thorn hollow or away from the escape route. He keeps his own wall for a breached flank; the druids avoid putting both walls across the same approach. The guardians can throw stones when bypassed, but moving them to preserve the screen exposes their guides.

Orris holds the +10-foot bank, while Mara directs the screen from the gully. The party can rush the northern root stair toward the cache or use the southern stair to take Orris from the flank; climbing between those routes can bypass the guardians at the cost of time and exposure. Orris uses Lunge near the bank lip when an enemy gives him an opening, making the drop a threat both sides can exploit. The trail-hands take opportunistic shots and use charcoal-burner smoke pots to cover exposed crossings or the mole's dash to the cache. They are hired interference, not lieutenants. One keeps calling the mole “officer,” enjoying how he flinches; if cornered, or shown proof, he would happily sell out his passenger for a clear retreat.

The mole spends the first moments trying to keep the party talking while edging toward the woodland path. When combat begins he moves toward the cache, and will not fight. His stolen ring gives him one helping hand for an escort (see The ring).

Special rules — included proposals

Read the field. At the start, point out the four elevation bands, root stairs, climbable ledges, stream, plank crossing, thorn hollow, cache and escape trail. Explain the guardians' bramble aura when branches first rise, before a player commits movement into it. The mole's struggle with his pack makes his escape preparations obvious.

Woodland mist. Beyond 5 squares, creatures have concealment from one another; within 5 squares, visibility is normal unless terrain says otherwise. This is a proposed abstraction for patchy mist, not total concealment or blocked line of sight. It affects both sides. Smoke does not add a second concealment penalty.

Elevation and access. Heights are feet above the streambed. The west shelf descends by root stairs at columns 6–7, rows 6–7 or 12–13. The eastern bank rises by broad root stairs through columns 12–13, rows 6–7 or 12–13, reaching +10 ft at column 14. The upper terrace has its own two-square-wide ascent: from row 7 (+10), through R at (15,6) or (16,6) (+15), to row 5 (+20). Each adjacent R-to-R connection crosses normally; the +5/+15 intermediate steps also connect normally to the +10/+20 ground immediately uphill. These routes require no checks, carry Large creatures and are not difficult terrain unless another effect makes them so.

Ledges and sight. Other brown edges are abrupt ledges equal to the printed height difference. As an encounter proposal, these root-covered faces can be climbed with Athletics DC 15 using normal climbing movement; forest walk does not bypass the climb. Use normal falling damage and forced-movement edge saves; a 5-foot drop inflicts no falling damage, while 10 feet deals 1d10 and 20 feet deals 2d10. A fall into the thorn hollow also triggers its terrain rule. Soil blocks sight through the bank: targets at an exposed lip can be seen from below, while targets set back may be hidden by the lip; adjudicate actual sight lines rather than granting every elevated creature cover. Height alone grants no attack bonus or extra range, and melee reach must span the vertical separation.

The mole's play for time. Before combat, he tries to keep them talking: “I sent the warning. Grappa's alive, isn't he? You got there?” “I gave Kell information. He was supposed to clear out before the raid. I didn't know what he'd do.” Mention the B-Team and he turns defensive: “If I'd known, I wouldn't have told him. What else do you want me to say?” Confronted about Grappa, he admits: “Yes. I gave them that address too. Before I heard.” Mara cuts in whenever the talk drifts to payment.

The ring. The mole wears the Ring of the Lingering Guide he took from the courier. Once during the encounter, as a minor action on his turn, he can choose a druid or guardian within 5 squares whom he can see; that creature shifts up to 2 squares as a free action. He uses it the first time a druid or guardian is bloodied, grabbed, or flanked, if the ring can pull it away from the fight. He shouts at that escort to clear the way to the exit. This demonstrates the ring's reward before the party earns it, and is a minor action only; he still cannot prepare his escape while an enemy is adjacent. If he surrenders, he drops the ring with his possessions. If he reaches E, he leaves with it and the party can recover it later when Delft's checkpoints catch him.

Shaken escorts. Orris and the trail-hands are not true believers. A PC within 5 squares can spend a standard action presenting proof (the anonymous warning, the forged reassignment order, Grappa's testimony, or the mole's own admissions) and make a Diplomacy or Intimidate check. Orris is shaken on DC 20; a trail-hand is shaken on DC 15, and one success shakes every trail-hand who can hear. A shaken escort stops attacking: Orris collapses his wall and withdraws along the trail, and each trail-hand flees. A failed check costs only the action and can be retried. Mara and the guardians cannot be shaken. Standing down does not change XP.

A fair chase. Because the mole is now two terraces above the gully, his escape kit is cinched to a rooted cache-frame. Before taking the kit, he must spend one additional standard action loosening it (three preparation actions total, on separate turns). A PC adjacent to O can still seize it with a minor action by cutting the straps. This visibly buys the party a round to navigate the new ascents; no escape timer runs before combat begins.

Stream and crossing. The stream and marked undergrowth are difficult terrain; the stream does not sweep creatures away. The two-square-wide plank crossing sits at +0 ft, is normal terrain and carries Large creatures; it crosses the water, not the banks. An adjacent creature can spend a standard action to lever out one plank square: change that square from = to ~. An occupied plank square cannot be removed this way. This needs no roll, harms nobody, and can hinder either side; the stream remains fordable everywhere.

Thorn hollow. A creature first entering a ^ square on a turn, or ending its turn there without having taken this damage on that turn, takes 5 damage. It is difficult terrain. The hollow is visible and affects both sides, including creatures with forest walk; overlapping thorn damage from a druid wall and this hollow applies only the higher terrain-damage instance that turn.

Escape preparation. D.1 acts on initiative 0, speed 6. He moves to O, then must spend three standard actions on separate turns loosening, retrieving and fitting his kit before he will attempt the marked exit. He cannot take these preparation actions while an enemy is adjacent. Announce each step; prepared escape happens only when he subsequently reaches E. A PC adjacent to O can take the kit with a minor action, preventing preparation until he recovers it. He can still move and hide if denied the kit, but will not bolt into unfamiliar mountains without it.

Apprehending the mole. If a PC reaches him while no conscious protector is within 3 squares, a standard action and a credible promise of protection make him surrender without a check. Otherwise, an adjacent PC can use a standard action with Diplomacy or Intimidate DC 20 to persuade him to surrender; a failed attempt can be retried after a protector falls or the kit is taken. Surrendered, he drops his possessions, stays put and accepts manacles; an adjacent PC can apply them with a standard action. For physical restraint before surrender, use the normal grab rules against Reflex 21; he escapes with Acrobatics +8 or Athletics +5 and does not attack. If needed: HP 25, AC 23, Fortitude 21, Reflex 21, Will 21; initiative 0, Perception +8. These are original noncombatant adjudication stats, not an XP-bearing monster.

Evidence and consequences. The mole's pack and purse hold his final payment, Grappa's address, a copy of the forged guard reassignment, and the courier's rendezvous instructions. Mara's route note shows the green-cord arrangement and the agreed price. Capturing the mole supplies testimony and accountability: he explains how he compromised Cippiano's operation and what else he sold. He knows little about the Obscurati beyond the courier's description and the handoff, so no new mandatory investigation opens before the summit. If he escapes, the documents still expose the betrayal and the party can recover him later through Delft's checkpoints. His role in the B-Team's deaths stays a matter for interrogation and evidence, not an automatic confession.

All special terrain and capture procedures above are encounter proposals for manual adjudication. No automatic forest fire, surprise damage, or extra wave is assumed.

Monster stats

A is a level-adjusted druid adaptation with revised monster math and softened wall control; C is an original human minion design, reflavoured from Kell lookouts to hired charcoal-road trail-hands. B retains its published mechanics, with renamed identity. Ability vignettes and names used for this encounter are adaptations. Ordinary standard monsters have no action points or saving throw bonus; all unlisted resistances and immunities are none.

Roots bend away as the guide slips between them.
Ignores difficult terrain when shifting.

The hooked staff cracks against your ribs.
Humanoid form only; melee 1, one creature; +17 vs. AC; hit: 3d6 + 10 damage.

Lunge — standard, at-will; melee basic.
The moss-backed hound catches your sleeve and hauls you toward the thorns.
Beast form only; melee 1, one creature; +15 vs. Reflex; hit: 3d6 + 10 damage. Effect: slide the target up to 2 squares.

Entangle — standard, at-will.
A twist of the staff pulls living roots around your ankles.
Ranged 5, one creature; +15 vs. Reflex; hit: immobilized (save ends).

Wall of Thorns — standard; available initially, recharges when first bloodied; zone.
A line of briars tears through the leaf mould, fencing you in with wet black spines.
Humanoid form only; area wall 4 within 10, up to 4 squares high; attack each creature in the wall, +15 vs. Reflex; hit: 2d6 + 3 damage and ongoing 5 damage (save ends). The wall lasts until encounter end and is difficult terrain to creatures without forest walk; it does not block sight. A creature without forest walk takes 5 damage the first time on a turn it enters the wall, or when it ends that turn there if it has not taken this zone damage on that turn. Multiple such walls share that 5-damage-per-turn cap. Each druid can maintain only one wall; creating another removes its previous wall.

Change Shape — minor, at-will; polymorph.
The guide drops to all fours, her coat collapsing into a pelt of moss and rain.
Change into a Medium beast, or return to humanoid form. Beast form persists until changed or death; other statistics remain the same.

Conversion: HP, defenses and attack bonuses follow the supplied level/role math; physical damage changes from 2d8+6 (15 average) to 3d6+10 (20.5). Wall hit changes from 1d10+5 (10.5) to 2d6+3 (10), retaining ongoing 5: nominal 15 per hit target after one ongoing tick, with a separate zone cost. Wall length is reduced from 8 to 4 and repeated terrain damage is capped to keep two controllers from overwhelming this capture fight. Ongoing duration remains save-dependent, so its actual output varies.
