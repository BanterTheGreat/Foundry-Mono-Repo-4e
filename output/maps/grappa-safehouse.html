The safehouse

Common room — south, rows 9–13. A rented place to sleep, eat, and sit quietly: rugs, a seating group at (3–4,10–11), and low luggage and bedding at (11–12,11–12). Tall packing cases at (7–8,11) split the approach and offer a place to break sight. Both broad northern doorways are visible from the entry area; the ransacking is immediately apparent.

Records room — northwest, columns 2–9, rows 2–7. Torn-open document cupboards line the walls. A desk, low document chests, and fallen drawers form the 2×2 clutter at (4–5,5–6). Grappa was reading here when they broke in; now he is strapped onto a seized luggage trolley at (7,5), with his lower body bound so he cannot walk. His unfinished replacement body and workshop equipment are absent.

Storeroom — northeast, columns 11–15, rows 2–7. Ordinary travel cases, provisions, and crates. Tall stacks occupy (12–13,2–3); low crates at (11–12,6–7) give cover without closing the approach. The rear loading doors at (16,3–4) open toward the waiting carriage.

What happened

Kell Clan intruders forced their way in and ransacked the safehouse for Grappa’s writings about the Bleak Gate project. They do not know about his geas: his written and tapped evasions sound like defiance, and neither intimidation nor handing him a pen bypasses the compulsion. Finding incomplete papers, they have bound him and are taking him away; the assassination order and eventual acid disposal remain in force.

At initiative, the searching has already happened. The papers are scattered and one gunman has a satchel of seized notes; searching does not secretly advance another clock or create extra enemy actions. Grappa has no voice here, so use written fragments and urgent tapping rather than shouted exposition.

Three routes through the fight

  • Direct rescue: advance through the common-room doors at (7–9,8) into the records room. The Gang Blade at (8,9) contests the approach but cannot occupy all three doorway squares. The western lookout supports it or falls back toward the records-room gunman.
  • Intercept the exit: go through (12–14,8) into the storeroom and head for the carriage door. The eastern lookout contests this route. The eastern gunman can fire down the room, net a closing rescuer, or move behind low crates; solid partitions give genuine sight breaks.
  • Flank through the connecting doors: the 15-foot opening at (10,4–6) links the two northern rooms, creating a loop. Enter one room and threaten the other rather than funneling everyone through a single choke point. The hauler uses the middle of this opening while escaping.

Enemy jobs

Bruiser: Haul first; tangle an interceptor if forced to fight. A lookout can replace it, consuming that lookout’s standard action. Gang Blade: delay the direct pursuit with its mark and melee jab, giving ground if flanked; the mark itself does not stop movement. Gunmen: cover different rooms and use their range-3 nets once rescuers close. Lookouts: screen the broad doorways, charge, flank, or take over the trolley. Once Grappa is freed and two standards are down, survivors flee or surrender if cornered.

Rescue and doors

Five-haul clock: the marked route from (7,5) through (10,5), across to (14,5), then via (14,4) and (15,4) to (16,4), is ten squares. Only one standard-action Haul per round, up to two squares, across the whole enemy group. Either rear doorway square is an escape threshold. A terrain-only diagonal route can be shorter, but still takes at least five Haul actions; do not advance the trolley automatically.

Hauling restrictions: hauler starts adjacent and accompanies the trolley, ending adjacent in a legal unoccupied square. It must be able to move and cannot be prone, immobilized, or restrained. Its movement provokes opportunity attacks normally; prevention stops the remaining movement. Neither trolley nor hauler passes through occupied or impassable squares.

Free Grappa: adjacent standard action, DC 19 Thievery or DC 23 Athletics. If no enemy is adjacent to him, no check is needed. Failure costs only the action; retry on a later turn. Suitable movement or teleportation powers can also rescue him under their normal targeting and carrying limits. After release, an adjacent ally can help him move two squares with a move action, accompanying him, only once per round. He is a conscious noncombatant objective; incidental area attacks do not destroy the rescue objective.

Doors: all broad openings start open. For this scenario, closing or reopening one whole double-door set costs an adjacent standard action; an unbarred door needs no check. An adjacent rescuer can close and bar the rear set with a standard action; an adjacent enemy needs a standard action and DC 23 Athletics to break the bar. Internal doors have no ready bar. Closed doors block sight and movement until opened or broken; adjudicate manually.

Ransacked clutter: papers, loose clothing, and spilled cases are difficult terrain. One standard action can topple one adjacent movable crate or low rack section, making that one square impassable and sight-blocking until righted with a standard action. Fixed seating and desks cannot be flipped using this shortcut.

Timing check: ignoring creatures and opportunity attacks, the checked approach from (8,13) to an adjacent rescue square costs 7 movement; the checked storeroom bypass from (10,13) to the inner rear threshold costs 9. These are terrain checks, not a guarantee against enemy interference. The two northern gunmen cannot shoot through solid partitions.

Unchanged roster: 1 bruiser, 1 blade, 2 gunmen, 2 minion lookouts; 2,700 enemy XP, Normal (+1). No extra enemies, no damaging hazards. The objective, doors, and terrain add pressure beyond the enemy-only XP calculation.
